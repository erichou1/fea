  \sub{SURROGATE TOPOLOGY OPTIMIZATION}
  \small\RaggedRight
  Classical SIMP iterates 100--1000 full FEA solves and cannot guarantee
  3D-printable output geometry. \textbf{SASTO} addresses all three failures:

  \vspace{2mm}
  \begin{minipage}[c]{0.41\linewidth}

    \Large\color{secbar}$\mathbf{K\,u = f}$
  \end{minipage}%
  \begin{minipage}[c]{0.57\linewidth}
    \small\RaggedRight
    \textbf{Linear elastostatics} governing equation.\\
    $K$ stiffness matrix, \;$u$ displacement, \;$f$ load.\\[2pt]
    Solved at data-generation time and for final validation.
    \textbf{Repeated FEA is eliminated during optimization.}
    SIMP solves it 100$+$ times per design.
  \end{minipage}

  \begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=3pt, topsep=0pt,

      infeasible at building scale ($128^3$ mesh, 19--77 min per solve).
    \item No guarantee of \textbf{topological connectivity} for 3D-printable

      unfabricable floating fragments.
    \item Uniform wall thickness \textbf{wastes material} in structurally

  \end{enumerate}

  FEA, 6-connectivity enforcement produces printable geometry, and part-aware
  thickness floors maximize material savings where structural demand is lowest.

%% L3: RESEARCH OBJECTIVES ────────────────────────────────
\sechdr{Research Objectives}
\begin{ccard}
  \small\RaggedRight
  \textbf{Engineering Problem:} SIMP requires 100s--1000s of costly FEA
  solves; existing surrogate models lack formal topology guarantees; and
  uniform-thickness constraints forego material savings in non-critical
  interior walls.\\[5pt]
  \textbf{Research Question:} Can a deep learning surrogate replace iterative
  FEA to enable fast, 3D-printable, and safety-verified topology optimization
  of full building structures, validated against independent FEA on 1,114
  held-out designs?\\[5pt]
  \textbf{Claim:} SASTO replaces iterative FEA with a \textbf{5-member deep
  ensemble} providing uncertainty-quantified structural predictions. It
  enforces \textbf{6-connected topology} for printability and achieves
  \textbf{23--92$\times$ speedup} with \textbf{zero safety violations} on
  1,114 test designs, verified by independent hex8 FEA.

  % ── Optimization objective ────────────────────────────────
  \sub{Optimization Objective}
  \small Minimize a three-term objective over the binary voxel field
  $\mathbf{p}$:
  \[\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\{0,1\}^N}
    J(\mathbf{p}) = w_V\!\tfrac{V}{V_0}
    + w_S\!\tfrac{S}{V_0} + P_\text{constraint}(\mathbf{p})\]
  $w_V{=}1.0$, $w_S{=}0.01$ (grid search). $P$ aggregates stress,
  compliance, and displacement violations, each normalized to $[0,1]$.

  \vspace{2mm}
  % ── Sensitivity score ─────────────────────────────────────
  \sub{Sensitivity Score}
  \small Voxel importance estimated via ensemble gradient:
  \[s_i = \tfrac{1}{M}\textstyle\sum_{m=1}^{M}
    \tfrac{\partial}{\partial p_i}
    \bigl[f_m(C) + 0.3\,f_m(\sigma)\bigr]\]
  {\color{teal}$s_i>0$: safe to remove.}\quad
  {\color{posterred}$s_i<0$: load-bearing, keep.}

% Row 1: Data Gen | Deep Ensemble
\begin{minipage}[t]{\HC}
  \sub{(1) Dataset Generation Pipeline}
  \fig{\IMG{fig_wireframe_pipeline.png}}{\linewidth}\\[-1pt]
  \fc{Fig.~2. Pipeline: 3DWire wireframe $\to$ boolean-fused solid $\to$
    hex8 FEA (SfePy) $\to$ $128^3$ voxel grid (7 input channels, 3 FEA
    labels).}

  \small\vspace{1mm}
  The 3DWire dataset provides \textbf{14,293 real building wireframe
  skeletons}. Each is processed through a custom pipeline: wall, roof, and
  floor solid extrusion, boolean union, then static FEA under ASCE 7-22
  gravity and wind load combinations using SfePy. Of 14,293 inputs,
  \textbf{3,115 (21.8\%)} are rejected for divergence, near-zero strain
  energy, or degenerate geometry, leaving \textbf{11,178 valid $128^3$
  grids}. A \textbf{family-aware split} clusters by architectural style to
  prevent near-duplicate leakage: \textbf{8,943 train / 1,121 val / 1,114
  test}. Raw FEA outputs: ${\sim}$200 GB; processed dataset: 59 GB. Targets
  span 4.9 orders of magnitude (stress) to 7.7 orders (compliance), requiring
  log-transform normalization and 2nd/98th-percentile winsorization.
\end{minipage}%
\hspace{2mm}%
\begin{minipage}[t]{\HC}
  \sub{(2) Deep Ensemble Surrogate (5$\boldsymbol{\times}$8.76 M params)}
  \fig{\IMG{Image 7.jpg}}{\linewidth}\\[-1pt]
  \fc{Fig.~3. Architecture: 4 strided conv stages (BatchNorm + GELU) $\to$
    3 SE-ResBlocks $\to$ dual global pool (avg + max) $\to$ 3 scalar
    outputs.}

  \small\vspace{1mm}
  Each of five independently-seeded members is a \textbf{3D convolutional
  network}. Four strided stages downsample the $128^3$ input; three
  Squeeze-and-Excitation ResBlocks capture long-range spatial dependencies;
  dual pooling (avg $+$ max) feeds three FC layers to predict stress,
  displacement, and compliance. The ensemble output is \textbf{mean $\mu$}
  and \textbf{std.\ dev.\ $\sigma$} per quantity, giving principled
  uncertainty bounds. Training: AdamW ($lr$=5e-4), Huber loss, cosine
  annealing, EMA ($\alpha$=0.999), random $90^\circ$ rotations and
  reflections. \textbf{Model v14} was the first to achieve substantially
  accurate validation predictions (compliance Spearman $\rho$=0.948).
\end{minipage}

  \sub{(3) SASTO Sensitivity-Guided Erosion}
  \fig{\IMG{fig_diag_optimization.png}}{\linewidth}\\[-1pt]
  \fc{Fig.~4. SASTO three-phase erosion loop: score voxels, select
    6-simple-point batch, tentatively remove, query ensemble, commit or
    undo with trust-region halving.}

  \small\vspace{1mm}
  The surrogate replaces FEA inside a voxel-removal loop. Each iteration:
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
    \item Compute distance transform; identify candidates deeper than
      $t_\text{min}(p)$.
    \item Score by ensemble sensitivity $s_i$; rank descending.
    \item Select batch $B$ of \textbf{6-simple-point} voxels
      (topology-safe, checked O(1) via Euler table).
    \item Remove tentatively, query ensemble. If $\hat{\sigma}^+ \leq
      \sigma_\text{allow}$ \textit{and} $\hat{C}/C_0 \leq 1.15$:
      \textbf{commit}. Otherwise: \textbf{undo} and halve $B$.
    \item \textbf{Phase 2} (endgame): fix $B$ at 5, then 1.
    \item \textbf{Phase 3}: swap corner voxels for face-adjacent
      topology-safe alternatives.
  \end{itemize}
  Post-process: pocket fill $\to$ SDF smoothing $\to$ Marching Cubes
  $\to$ STL export.

  \eqpill{$\hat{\sigma}^+ = \mu_\sigma + k\sigma_\sigma$,
    \quad $\hat{C}^+ = \mu_C + k\sigma_C$,
    \quad $k=1.0$ at operating point.}
\end{minipage}%
\hspace{2mm}%
\begin{minipage}[t]{\HC}
  \sub{(4) Topology: 6-Connectivity Guarantee}
  \begin{minipage}[c]{0.47\linewidth}\centering
    \fig{\IMG{Image 9.jpg}}{13.3cm}\\[-1pt]
    \fc{{\color{posterred}26-conn: floating fragments}}
  \end{minipage}\hfill
  \begin{minipage}[c]{0.47\linewidth}\centering
    \fig{\IMG{Image 10.jpg}}{13.3cm}\\[-1pt]
    \fc{{\color{teal}6-conn: single component}}
  \end{minipage}

  \vspace{1.5mm}\small
  \textit{Proposition (Kong \& Rosenfeld 1989): A binary voxel field with
  exactly one 6-connected foreground component yields a single-component
  Marching Cubes mesh.}

  \vspace{1mm}
  The classical 26-adjacency criterion permits isolated islands attached
  only at diagonal corners, which become floating geometry after meshing
  and \textbf{cannot be fabricated by any 3D printing toolpath}. SASTO
  enforces the \textbf{6-simple-point} criterion: a voxel is removed only
  if deletion leaves the 6-connected component count unchanged, checked
  in O(1) via a 26-neighborhood Euler characteristic lookup table.

  \vspace{2mm}
  \textbf{Verification (60 designs):}
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
    \item {\color{teal}100\%} single-component voxel fields guaranteed
    \item {\color{teal}90\%} single-component meshes without post-processing
    \item {\color{teal}10\%} resolved by one connected-component fill pass
    \item {\color{teal}Zero} cases required geometry discard
  \end{itemize}

\begin{ccard}
  \small\RaggedRight

  \fig{\IMG{fig_k_factor.png}}{\linewidth}\\[-1pt]
  \fc{Fig.~15. $k$-factor Pareto: acceptance rate (teal, left axis) vs.\
    mean volume reduction (gold, right axis). Operating point $k$=1.0.}

  \small\vspace{1mm}
  The scalar $k$ sets the heuristic uncertainty margin in
  $\hat{y}^+=\mu+k\sigma$:
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=1pt]
    \item Low $k$: aggressive removal; higher violation risk
    \item High $k$: safer, but near-zero headroom for many designs
    \item \textbf{Operating point $k{=}1.0$}: ${>}99\%$ acceptance rate;
      heuristic $1\sigma$ buffer. Conformal 84.1\% one-sided $k$=1.90.
  \end{itemize}

%% R2: CONCLUSIONS ────────────────────────────────────────
\sechdr{Conclusions}
\begin{ccard}
  \small\RaggedRight
  SASTO is the \textbf{first system combining} (a) building-scale 3D-CNN
  surrogate inference, (b) 6-connected topology enforcement for printability,
  and (c) conformal distribution-free safety coverage for structural topology
  optimization.\\[4pt]
  \textbf{All three engineering endpoints are met:}
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=3pt, topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Speed:} 50\,s median vs.\ SIMP 19--77\,min.
      {\color{teal}Endpoint met.}
    \item \textbf{Printability:} 100\% single-component voxel fields; 90\%
      direct single-component meshes. {\color{teal}Endpoint met.}
    \item \textbf{Safety:} 0/1,114 same-method FEA compliance-ratio
      violations; conformal upper bound 0.950 vs.\ limit 1.15.
      {\color{teal}Endpoint met and exceeded.}
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{3mm}
  \textbf{Practical Impact.}
  A median 23.5\% concrete reduction per building translates to millions of
  tonnes of avoided CO$_2$ annually at scale. SASTO runs on a single consumer
  GPU in under one minute, enabling engineers to explore dozens of design
  variants in a morning, a workflow impossible with SIMP at $128^3$.

  \vspace{2mm}

    left=3mm,right=3mm,top=2mm,bottom=2mm,width=\linewidth]

    \textbf{8\% of global CO$_2$ is cement.}
    Even 10\% adoption of AI-optimized construction would offset
    \textbf{tens of millions of tonnes annually.}
  \end{tcolorbox}
\end{ccard}

%% R3: FUTURE WORK ────────────────────────────────────────
\sechdr{Future Work}
\begin{ccard}
  \small\RaggedRight
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=3pt, topsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{FEA-in-the-loop active learning:} When ensemble $\sigma$
      exceeds a threshold mid-run, trigger a ground-truth FEA solve and
      fine-tune on the result, providing a self-correcting safety net.
    \item \textbf{Nonlinear material validation:} Apply concrete damaged
      plasticity FEA to assess cracking and buckling in 78\,mm interior
      partitions under extreme loads.
    \item \textbf{Physical print test:} 3D-print one optimized house at
      1:10 scale in micro-concrete; apply compression to failure; compare
      DIC strain fields to FEA predictions.
    \item \textbf{Seismic and wind:} ASCE 7-22 Chapter 12 seismic zone
      compliance extension.
    \item \textbf{Multi-material:} Geopolymer or fiber-reinforced concrete
      in thin sections for higher strength-to-weight ratio.
    \item \textbf{Other domains:} Ship hulls, aircraft ribs, biomedical
      scaffolds; all share the same boundary-value problem structure.
  \end{itemize}
\end{ccard}

%% R3b: BOUNDARY CONDITIONS & LIMITS ────────────────────────
\sechdr{Boundary Conditions \& Limits}
\begin{ccard}
  \small\RaggedRight
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=3pt, topsep=0pt]
    \item Single-story residential buildings only (3DWire dataset scope)
    \item Linear-elastic concrete model; no cracking or plasticity
    \item Gravity + wind loading (ASCE 7-22); seismic not modeled
    \item Fixed min-$x$ boundary condition throughout
    \item \textbf{Not} a full ACI\,318 or code-compliant structural check
    \item Nonlinear cracking, construction tolerances not modeled
    \item Generalization to multi-story, other materials, or larger
      footprints not yet verified
  \end{itemize}
\end{ccard}
